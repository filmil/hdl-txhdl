\datasheet{Timer}{Vreteno's core-local interruptor: a count, and a compare, a software interrupt and two lines for each of two harts, with a mailbox, behind an AXI peripheral end.}

\dsfacts{\code{cpu/vreteno/src/timer.rs}}{\code{vreteno32::timer::Timer}}%
{\code{//docs:vreteno}, \code{//docs:axi}}

\subsection*{Function}

\code{Timer} is the core-local interruptor of the Vreteno machine, the
CLINT. It holds a 64-bit count, \code{mtime}, and for each of two
harts a 64-bit compare and one bit: \code{mtimecmp} and \code{msip}
for hart 0, \code{mtimecmp1} and \code{msip1} for hart 1 (issue 1408).
It raises \code{tirq} while the count has reached hart 0's compare,
which the core reads as bit 7 of \code{mip}, and it raises \code{sirq}
while \code{msip} is set, which the core reads as bit 3. \code{tirq1}
and \code{sirq1} are hart 1's two lines, from its own compare and its
own bit. A program raises a hart's software interrupt by writing that
hart's bit and clears it by writing it back to zero; nothing else
touches those bits. The count goes out on \code{time}, which the core
reads (issue 1012).

The registers are at the offsets every RISC-V platform puts them at,
so that a stock port of an operating system finds them where it
looks: hart 1's \code{msip} at \code{0x0004} beside hart 0's at
\code{0x0000}, and hart 1's compare at \code{0x4008} beside hart 0's
at \code{0x4000}. That is what makes the window 64\,KiB:
\code{mtime} sits at \code{0xbff8}, far from the compares, and the
two cannot share a page.

The mailbox is two more words, \code{mbox\_entry} at \code{0xc000} and
\code{mbox\_arg} at \code{0xc004}. A program writes them and the
other hart reads them: hart 1's boot memory, \code{park.rs}, waits
for its \code{msip}, then reads where to start from \code{0xc000} and
what with from \code{0xc004}. The timer itself does nothing with them.

It is the peripheral client of an \code{AxiPer}, and its link is one
port, \code{bus}, a \code{PerPort} of the four channels: requests
come on \code{bus\_req} and write beats on \code{bus\_w}, and answers
go out on \code{bus\_ans} and \code{bus\_r}. The input \code{rst}
clears the count and sets both compares to all ones (issue 419), and
does nothing else.

\subsection*{Register map}

Offsets from the controller's base, which is
\code{isa::CLINT\_BASE}, \code{0x0200\_0000}, under the mask
\code{isa::CLINT\_MASK}, \code{0xffff\_0000}: a window of 64\,KiB.

\dsregs{Timer}

The map is declared as \code{clint}, with fourteen select bits: an
address's bits 15 to 2 select the word. A word is addressed only at its
first byte. An address with either low bit set selects no word, so it
reads zero and takes no write, as the reference model has it, and a
store of a byte or a half to a register's second lane is dropped. A
word the map does not name reads zero, and a write to it is dropped
(issue 681). The other 31 bits of a write to \code{msip} or
\code{msip1} are dropped and read back as zero.

The controller selects the word by the whole 16-bit offset, bits 15 to
0, and looks at no bit above them: the router sends it only the bursts
in its window, so nothing higher has to be decoded. The offsets are far
apart, which is why the decode is on the offset rather than on two bits
of it. The map names ten words in the 64\,KiB, and every other word
reads zero and takes no write.

\dstables{Timer}

\subsection*{Behaviour}

\begin{itemize}
\item The count adds one every cycle. With \code{rst} high it goes to
zero.
\item A read is taken when \code{bus\_r} has room, no write is waiting
for its beat and no refused burst is being answered. A single beat is
answered on \code{bus\_r} in the cycle it is taken, with the word
addressed, \code{OKAY} and \code{last} set.
\item A write is taken when no write is waiting. The map's write
enables for the word it names, one bit a register, go into
\code{pwe}, and its identifier into \code{pid}.
\item The held write's beat is taken when it has arrived and, for the
last beat, \code{bus\_ans} has room. The lanes the strobe covers
replace those bytes of the word, the other bytes stay, and
\code{bus\_ans} sends \code{OKAY}.
\item A burst of more than one beat is refused (issue 1208). A read
gets every beat it asked for, each \code{SLVERR} with zero data, the
last with \code{last} set; \code{rleft} and \code{rid} count them out.
A write has every beat taken and nothing written, then one
\code{SLVERR}.
\item Every cycle \code{pending} takes whether \code{mtime} is at or
above \code{mtimecmp}, and \code{pending1} whether it is at or above
\code{mtimecmp1}, compared as unsigned numbers. \code{tirq} is
\code{pending} and \code{tirq1} is \code{pending1}, each a register's
output, so a core sees in one cycle what the timer decided in the
cycle before.
\item \code{rst} sets both compares to all ones, so a compare left by
the previous program does not fire once the count reaches it
(issue 419).
\item \code{sirq} is \code{msip} itself and \code{sirq1} is
\code{msip1}, with no register between, so a core sees a software
interrupt in the cycle the write lands rather than the cycle after.
Nothing but a write to \code{0x0000} or \code{0x0004} changes those
bits: the controller neither sets them nor clears them.
\item The count is driven every cycle, and a write to one of its halves
is driven after that, so a write in the same cycle as a tick keeps the
written word and the tick is lost.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Verification}

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{//cpu/vreteno:lockstep\_test} hands \code{pending} and
\code{msip} to the model for each instruction and checks
\code{mtimecmp} against the model at the halt, after draining the bus,
since a store is posted and the compare lands some cycles after the
core issued it. \code{demo\_program} sets the compare to 150 and counts
the timer's interrupt. \code{random\_programs} reads the count and
writes the compare 64 cycles beyond it, in sixty-four seeded programs.
\item \code{a\_program\_that\_interrupts\_itself} writes
\code{msip}, takes the trap, clears the bit in the handler and counts;
it is the software interrupt's own test.
\item In \code{timer.rs}, in \code{//cpu/vreteno:isa\_test},
\code{a\_write\_of\_one\_to\_msip\_raises\_it} and
\code{a\_write\_to\_an\_unused\_offset}\allowbreak
\code{\_leaves\_msip\_alone} drive the
controller alone, and \code{hart\_ones\_words\_and\_the\_mailbox}
writes hart 1's \code{msip} at \code{0x0004}, which raises its line
and not hart 0's, and reads back its compare at \code{0x4008} and the
mailbox at \code{0xc000} and \code{0xc004}.
\item \code{//cpu/vreteno:pair\_test} and \code{//cpu/vreteno:board\_test}
run the controller on a bus beside a core, though neither is about it.
In \code{board\_test}, \code{hart\_one\_starts\_twice}\allowbreak
\code{\_from\_the\_mailbox}
starts hart 1 through the mailbox and its \code{msip}.
\item The build simulates \code{Timer<2>}'s netlist against the trace
of \code{//cpu/vreteno:vreteno} under nvc and Verilator:
\code{//docs:vreteno\_sim\_timer\_timer\_tb\_test} and
\code{//docs:vreteno\_vsim\_timer\_test}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Used by}

\code{Board}, as \code{timer} behind the tracker \code{ptimer}, with
\code{tirq} and \code{sirq} wired to the inputs of those names of
\code{cpu}, hart 0, \code{tirq1} and \code{sirq1} to those of
\code{cpu1}, hart 1, and \code{time} to both, in the 64\,KiB from
\code{0x0200\_0000}. The demonstration
\code{//cpu/vreteno:vreteno}, the lockstep test and \code{run.rs}.

\subsection*{Limits}

The counter increments once per clock cycle. The controller answers
single beats only: a burst of more than one beat is refused with
\code{SLVERR}, and \code{size} is not inspected. Two harts and no
more. Address decoding ignores bits above bit 15 because
the interconnect router filters traffic outside the peripheral's 64\,KiB window.
One write request is held at a time, during which incoming reads stall.
Writes update 32 bits of a 64-bit register per access, requiring two store
instructions to update a full compare threshold. The counter and compare registers
reside beyond the 12-bit immediate displacement range of a single base register,
requiring separate base pointers in software routines (\code{program.rs}).
A word the map does not name answers \code{OKAY}, not a decode error.
