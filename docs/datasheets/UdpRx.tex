\datasheet{UdpRx}{A UDP/IPv4 receive filter after the MAC's receiver:
the payload of each datagram addressed to it, and nothing else.}

\dsfacts{\code{lib/parts/src/udprx.rs}}{\code{txhdl\_parts::udprx::UdpRx}}%
{\code{//docs:datasheets}}

\subsection*{Function}

\code{UdpRx} takes the frames \code{EthRx} offers: only frames whose
check sequence held, without it, from the destination address on,
with the last byte marked, and a short frame still padded to sixty
bytes. It passes on the payload of each UDP datagram addressed to the
MAC address, IPv4 address and UDP port its configuration names, and
nothing of any other frame.

Its inputs are the frames, \code{Rx<EthByte>}, and its configuration,
\code{In<UdpCfg>}. \code{UdpCfg} holds \code{mac}, a 48-bit MAC
address with its first byte on the wire in bits 47 to 40; \code{ip},
the IPv4 address or the multicast group it listens to, its first byte
in bits 31 to 24; and \code{port}. Its outputs are the payloads,
\code{Tx<EthByte>}, and a count of the datagrams passed on,
\code{Out<U<32>>}.

Every header byte is checked as it goes by, and none is held. The
checks are done by the forty-second byte, \code{HEADER}, where the
payload starts, and nothing is sent before it, so a frame that fails
a check gives no byte at all.

\subsection*{Parameters}

None. The tables are generated for \code{UdpRx}.

\subsection*{Behaviour}

The checks, by the frame's byte offset:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0 to 5: the destination address is the configured one. When the
configured IPv4 address is a multicast group, 224.0.0.0/4, it is the
group's instead: \code{01:00:5e} and the group's low 23 bits.
Broadcast is refused.
\item 12 and 13: the type is IPv4, \code{0x0800}. A VLAN tag is
refused.
\item 14: \code{0x45}, version 4 and a header of five words. Options
are refused.
\item 16 and 17: the total length is at least the UDP length and the
twenty bytes of the IPv4 header.
\item 20 and 21: more-fragments is clear and the fragment offset is
zero. Don't-fragment is ignored.
\item 23: the protocol is 17, UDP.
\item 14 to 33: the header checksum. The ones' complement sum over the
header, taken a pair of bytes at a time, is all ones.
\item 30 to 33: the destination address is the configured one.
\item 36 and 37: the destination port is the configured one.
\item 38 and 39: the UDP length is at least nine. An empty datagram
has no byte to mark last, so it is refused.
\end{itemize}

The payload and the count:

\begin{itemize}
\item The payload is the UDP length less eight bytes. Its last byte
goes out marked last. If the frame ends first, the frame's last byte
is marked instead, and the datagram is cut short there.
\item What follows the payload, padding included, is taken and
dropped.
\item The count rises with each datagram's last byte, and wraps.
\item A payload byte is taken from the receiver only when the output
is ready. A header byte, or a byte being dropped, is taken whenever it
is offered.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{//lib/parts:parts\_test} runs \code{udprx::tests}:
\begin{itemize}

checks the tests' own checksum against a published IPv4 header;

from a fixed seed, most valid but for one field, against a reference
filter in Rust, with the output taken on two cycles in three;
\item \code{it\_lowers}, which writes its Verilog.
\end{itemize}
\item Its netlist is not simulated against a trace.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Limits}

It does not take IPv4 options, fragments or a VLAN tag, and it
refuses broadcast. It does not check the source address or port, the
time to live or the UDP checksum. The UDP checksum covers the payload,
so checking it would mean holding the whole datagram, and the MAC's
CRC-32 has already checked every byte of the frame. It receives only:
there is no sending side.
