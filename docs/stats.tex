\section{What Was Counted}

Every file tracked by git in the repository \code{HDL/txhdl}, outside
\code{third\_party/}, was counted with \code{wc -l}, so comments and
blank lines count as lines. Lock files and JSON (\code{Cargo.lock},
\code{MODULE.bazel.lock}, \code{cargo-bazel-lock.json},
\code{multitool.lock.json} and the like) and \code{.bazelversion} were
excluded, because they are generated artifacts, not the project's own
writing. \code{third\_party/} holds vendored \LaTeX{} packages, the IEEE
document class and other code under their own licenses, and is not this
project's work.

The measurement was taken on October 27, 2026, at commit
\code{489a2273} of the mainline, with the script of
Listing~\ref{lst:stats-script}; executed from the root of a checkout,
it reports Tables~\ref{tab:stats-topic} and~\ref{tab:stats-lang} for
that checkout. The topics are the directories of the tree, named in
the script. The tree outgrew the script's topics between the two
editions: the GL ES library, the Linux port, FireSim, the demos, the
runtime's tests and the datasheets had none, and 27,000 lines fell into
the root's row. This edition names them (issue 1989), and the script's
own lines are counted as they were at \code{489a2273}.

\section{What the Numbers Say}

Rust is nearly two lines in three: 166,884 of 262,507. The language,
its runtime and the lowering macro together, is 24,579 lines, under a
tenth of the tree. Most of the Rust is written \emph{in} the language
rather than \emph{for} it: the parts library, the Vreteno core, Razboj
and the board subsystems are about 70,000 lines of hardware, and the
GL ES library, the programs the core runs and the Zephyr and Linux
ports are about 41,000 more of software for that hardware.

The parts library is now the largest topic, 32,621 lines in 64 files,
larger than the runtime and the macro together. Its largest files are
the AXI link, at 2,208 lines, the SD controller and the scanout.

Checking is a quarter of the Rust. The 147 example files, 23,492
lines, are each a test, and with the Vreteno tests, the runtime's
tests, the probes, and the conformance and layout runs they come to
about 42,000 lines, against the 70,000 of hardware they check.

Prose kept up, though not in step. The documents, the datasheets, the
design notes and the early specifications are 43,113 lines, a quarter
of the Rust; at the first edition they were nearly as long as it.
The documents still include their listings from the tree, so these are
lines of explanation, not copies of code.

Tooling is 16,582 lines in 122 files, two thirds of it Rust. The
largest tool is no longer a testbench generator but \code{bootimg},
which builds the Linux boot image and the inflater that unpacks it on
the core, followed by \code{datasheet}, which writes every datasheet's
tables from the lowering.

\section{Since September}
\label{sec:stats-growth}

Table~\ref{tab:stats-growth} sets this edition beside the first. The
44 days between them saw 3,297 commits. The tree grew eightfold, and
its Rust elevenfold. The language grew fourfold, and what is built in
it far faster: the Vreteno core is six times its size, the parts
library went from three files and 158 lines to 32,621, and Razboj, the
GL ES library and the Zephyr and Linux ports did not exist. The
early specifications are the one topic that did not move, as a record
of where the language began.
