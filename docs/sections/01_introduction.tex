\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{T}{wo} hardware description languages were written for one
project, independently, and both describe the same intended hardware. The
goal is a single consistent specification. This article states what has to
happen to get there, and settles every question that a merge cannot leave
open.

The short answer is that the two languages do not compete. LHDL says how a
large system is assembled, configured and scheduled. TxHDL says how one
module talks a multi-cycle protocol. Each is nearly silent where the other
is detailed. Four questions need a decision about meaning rather than about
spelling, and Section~\ref{sec:conflicts} resolves all four.

\subsection{What the sources are}

Seven files state language content. Their sizes say which ones matter.

\code{workspace/spec}        &  486 & LHDL  & condensed, with a grammar \\

\code{theses.md}             &   94 & LHDL  & twelve design theses \\

The most complete specification in the repository was, until this work, the
one that was never built or published. \code{spec/language.md} had no build
target, while the four smaller files did.

The language is named four ways across those files. The long LHDL draft
writes FLHDL 88 times, the condensed one writes it 9 times, the earliest
sketch writes LHdl 6 times, and \code{spec/language.md} writes TxHDL 5
times. The repository, the build module and the published path are all named
\code{txhdl}. Section~\ref{sec:decisions} settles the name.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item An account of what each language actually specifies, and of the
        asymmetry that makes a merge possible rather than a compromise
        (Section~\ref{sec:two-languages}).
  \item Resolutions for the four semantic conflicts, of which the model of
        time is the one that shapes the language
        (Section~\ref{sec:conflicts}).
  \item A surface syntax biased toward Rust, decided construct by
        construct, which removes six defects present in the sources rather
        than restating them (Section~\ref{sec:syntax}).
  \item An inventory of every defect found by reading the two sources,
        with line numbers, including sixteen places where the LHDL grammar
        and the LHDL examples disagree
        (Section~\ref{sec:defects}).
\end{enumerate}
