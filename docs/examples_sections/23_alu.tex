\section{An ALU}
\label{sec:x-alu}

The ten operations of an ALU, one per arm of a \code{case!}: the
arithmetic, the bitwise logic and the shifts are Rust's operators on
\code{U<N>}, Section~\ref{sec:x-ops}, and the two that have no
operator are functions, \code{sra}, the arithmetic shift, and
\code{lt\_signed}, the signed compare, lowered as \code{\$signed} in
Verilog and \code{signed} in VHDL. \code{op} selects with \code{case!}
over an enum, the result is the register \code{r} so the \code{case!}
can drive it, and the output wire \code{y} shows it. A compare yields
a truth value; \code{Bit::from} makes it a bit and \code{zext} the
datapath's width, and the lowering drops both calls, since the
target's width is the extension.
The testbench drives ten operations from a table, one per cycle, and
the lowering is simulated against that trace,
Section~\ref{sec:x-checked}. Concatenation and sign extension are on
\code{U<N>} too, \code{concat::<K, M>} and \code{sext::<M>}, with the
result width stated as \code{mul::<M>} states it.
