\section{A Whole Link Made in One Line}
\label{sec:x-link}

A unit of units makes the channels between its children, and it made
them one at a time: a \code{chan::<T, C>()} per channel, each with a
pair of names. An AXI-Lite link is five channels, so it was five
lines and ten names, and a board with a dozen links between its
bridges and its peripherals was a hundred and twenty.

\code{link::<B>()} makes a whole bundle. \code{B} is one side's
struct of ports, here \code{LitePort}, what a peripheral holds, and
what comes back is the other side's struct, \code{LiteHostPort}, and
\code{B}: the same five channels by the same names with each end
turned round. A struct of ports that can be made this way implements
\code{Link}, which names the other side's struct and makes the two.
In the netlist each channel is a net named for the link and the
field, so the one below is \code{host\_aw}, \code{host\_ar} and the
rest. Each side is passed on whole, which joins its channels in the
order the struct declares them, or a channel at a time as
\code{host.aw}, which a bridge that takes its channels in two tuples
needs.

The macro cannot see how many channels a bundle has, so it cannot
check that each is joined at both ends. When the unit is lowered,
every channel between its children is counted, and one that joins
anything but one sender and one receiver is refused.

\code{Relay} forwards each channel of a link from one side to the
other, as a register slice does, and \code{Word} is one word behind a
link. The parent takes the link from the testbench, passes it to the
relay, and joins the relay to the word with a link it makes whole.
The build checks the parent's netlist against the run under nvc and
Verilator.
