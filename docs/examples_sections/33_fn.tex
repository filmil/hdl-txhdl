\section{A Function, Inlined}
\label{sec:x-fn}

The step of a unit is one loop body, and a long one reads as a wall.
A function under \code{\#[lower]} takes a piece of it out under a
name. It is combinational, a few \code{let}s and a value; it is plain
Rust for the simulation; and it is inlined at every call in a
lowered unit of the same file, its parameters bound to the arguments
and its \code{let}s to expressions of their own. The lowering reads
the file for it, since a proc macro sees one item at a time. What it
can inline is what the step could have said in place, and nothing
else: no register, no port, no \code{with!}. The counter here keeps
its three pieces of arithmetic so, the Gray code of the count,
whether the count is about to wrap, and its two nibbles swapped, and
the netlist is the one the step would have made with them written in.

A tuple \code{let}, which the third of them uses, is taken apart into
the \code{let}s it stands for. It has to be: a helper's \code{let} is
a substitution rather than a wire, so a name of \code{(lo, hi)} would
substitute for nothing and leave \code{lo} and \code{hi} in the
netlist with nothing declaring them. VHDL then refuses to analyse and
Verilog quietly makes them one-bit wires, which is the worse of the
two. A tuple whose value is not a tuple of the same length, such as a
call that returns a pair, is refused where the helper is called, with
the probe for it kept in \code{experiments/rust\_embedding}.
