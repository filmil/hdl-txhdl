\section{An Offer Taken on Its Edge}
\label{sec:x-put}

A sequence that walks a store and offers its words has to move its
address exactly on the edge a word is taken. A step later, and the
next offer reads the word it has just given; a step early, and while
the consumer stalls the offer changes under it.
\code{tx.put(|| v).await} says it (issue 755): it waits for an edge at
which the channel has room, sends in the step after it, and what
follows it happens in that step, so a register set after it takes its
value on the take. It is the sender's side of \code{rx.wait()}, and
it is \code{until(C::rising, || tx.ready().to\_bool())} and then
\code{tx.send(v)}, in that order. The word is a closure, read at the
edge as \code{until} reads its condition, since a word read where
\code{put} is called is read before the address has moved. In the
netlist the offer's \code{valid} is the state's condition, and the
state is left on \code{ready}.

The walker takes eight words into a store, one a cycle as they come,
and then puts them out in order, its read address \code{at} moving on
each take. The address is one register and the store's only read
port, which is what lets a larger store be a RAM, as the Ethernet
receiver's is. Its consumer here takes when a small shift register
says so, so an offer is held a cycle or several before it goes; the
run asserts that all sixteen words come back once and in order, and
the netlist is checked against the run, stalls and all, under nvc and
Verilator.
