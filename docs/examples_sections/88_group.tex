\section{Port Groups}
\label{sec:x-group}

A unit with many wires to the outside, a board with its pins, used to
list each one as a port of its own: \code{rst: In<Bit>},
\code{irq: In<Bit>} and so on, the wrapper repeated on every line, and
the clock domain written again on each wire of the domain. A port
group puts them in one struct of plain values (issue 1647). With
\code{\#[value(ports)]} beside \code{\#[derive(Value)]}, the struct is
one port of the unit, \code{In<Ctl>} or \code{Out<Stat>}, and its clock
domain is written once, as \code{In<Ctl, C>}. A struct without the
marker is still one packed port, as a request that travels whole
should be.

The netlist gives a group one port per field, named for the port and
the field, \code{ctl\_en} for the field \code{en} of the port
\code{ctl}. With \code{\#[value(ports, bare)]} the fields keep their
own names, for pins whose names a board already gives them, such as
\code{lamp} and \code{beat} here. Inside the module the whole is a
wire under the port's name, joined to the field ports at the module's
edge, so a unit of one loop reads and drives the whole as it would any
value: \code{Panel} reads \code{ctl.get()} and sets \code{stat} whole.

A unit of units hands each child its own field. \code{split()} gives
an end per field, a struct of \code{In} or \code{Out} named for the
group with \code{Ins} or \code{Outs} after it, and \code{Desk} passes
\code{k.en}, under the name \code{en}, to one lamp and \code{k.clear}
to the other, each driving
its own field of \code{leds}. In simulation the end of a field reads or
drives that field of the whole wire at once, so splitting a group
changes nothing about when a value is seen. Both netlists are checked
against the run under nvc and Verilator, each field port against that
field of the trace.
