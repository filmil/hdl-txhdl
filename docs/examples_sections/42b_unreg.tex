\section{Unregistered Channels}
\label{sec:x-unreg}

A channel between two units is registered on both sides, so each one a
transaction crosses costs a cycle. \code{\#[unregistered]} on the
\code{let} that makes a channel in a unit of units takes that cycle
away: while the channel is empty, the receiver sees the sender's
\code{valid} and \code{data} in the cycle they are offered, and takes
the transaction then; one it does not take waits in the channel as
before. \code{ready} is still a register, so nothing runs from the
receiver back to the sender (issue 1293).

\code{ex\_unreg} builds one chain twice. A forwarding stage hands a
request to a stage that answers from a register, and a second
forwarding stage hands the answer on. In \code{Chain} the two channels
inside are registered; in \code{ChainU} they are unregistered, and the
chain answers its first request two cycles sooner. Both take a request
a cycle. The sink stops taking for three cycles in the middle, so the
answers back up and an unregistered channel keeps an offer it was not
taken, and both chains still deliver every answer in order.

The answering stage holds its answer in a register, and that is what
lets both of its channels be unregistered: without it, the two would
join the forwarding stages into one path of wires end to end. Each
child is joined before the one it sends to, since the receiver of an
unregistered channel has to run after its sender in the step; joined
the other way round, the run stops and names the channel. Both
netlists are checked against this run under both simulators, the
stall included.
