\section{A Counted Step in a Process of Several Waits}
\label{sec:x-serial}

Section~\ref{sec:x-seq} numbered a loop's waits into states. A step
that repeats a fixed number of times, a bit a cycle for eight cycles,
is a \code{for} whose body waits, and the lowering counts it: the
body's waits are states once, and a register the unit does not
declare, \code{for0} for the first such loop, counts the turns.
Entering the loop sets the counter to the low bound. The statements
before the body's first wait, which set a turn up, happen in the
state before the loop with the variable read as the low bound; the
body's states read the variable as the counter, so \code{bit(i)} is
a bit selected by a register; the body's last state goes back to its
first while the counter is short of the last value, adding one, with
the set-up happening there and the variable read as the counter plus
one, and goes on otherwise. So \code{for i in 0..8} around one wait
is one state and a three-bit counter, whatever the bound.

The bound may then be anything the netlist can evaluate when
\code{lowered} runs, a const parameter included. One shape is not
counted: a body whose waits are all under \code{if} may take no
edge at all on a turn, which a counter that moves once an edge
cannot follow, so such a loop is unrolled, a turn at a time, and its
bounds are numbers written out. A \code{for} that does not wait
keeps the unrolling of Section~\ref{sec:x-loop}, which happens when
\code{lowered} runs; the shapes are told apart by whether the body
waits, and where. A wait inside a \code{for} was dropped without a
word before any of this, one clocked block doing every turn at once
(issue 587).

The serialiser below takes a byte and sends its bits, low bit first,
one a cycle. Two states, the wait for the byte and the turn, and the
counter. Its netlist shows what the counting did: the bit channel's
data is the bit the counter selects, and the turn's state goes back
to itself seven times. The build checks the netlist against the run
under nvc and Verilator.
